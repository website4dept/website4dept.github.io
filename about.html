Department of Mathematics · Since 1951

The oldest mathematics department in the IIT system.

Founded alongside IIT Kharagpur in 1951, the Department of Mathematics brings together pure mathematics, applied mathematics, probability & statistics and theoretical computer science in one research and teaching community.

Overview

About the Department

The Department of Mathematics was established in 1951 through the vision and efforts of the late Prof. B. R. Seth, its first Head. Under his leadership the department built a strong school of Continuum Mechanics that went on to supply faculty and experts to many newer institutions across the country. In 1956 Prof. Seth founded the Indian Society of Theoretical and Applied Mechanics (ISTAM), which earned international recognition.

In the decades that followed, the department grew to include expertise in statistics and quality control, operations research, reliability, algebra, complex and functional analysis, optimisation and database systems. Responding to the needs of a growing software industry, it introduced the M.Tech. in Computer Science and Data Processing and the Integrated M.Sc. in Mathematics and Computing — programmes that were the first of their kind in India and that brought mathematics and theoretical computer science together.

Today, building on its foundation in applied mathematics, the department has balanced strength across four broad areas: Pure Mathematics, Applied Mathematics, Probability & Statistics and Theoretical Computer Science. Faculty and research scholars also work closely with other departments of the Institute on problems in microscale transport, cyber security, quantum computing, image processing, data science and statistical simulation.

Research Area 01

Pure Mathematics

The pure mathematics groups pursue fundamental questions in algebra, analysis, geometry and topology. The department has long-standing strength in commutative algebra, combinatorics and graph theory, topology and functional analysis.

Research spans algebraic geometry, homological and homotopical algebra, algebraic K-theory, Lie algebras and associative algebras; analytic number theory and modular forms; operator theory, abstract harmonic analysis and harmonic analysis on Lie groups; geometric function theory, quasiconformal and planar harmonic mappings; and algebraic and geometric topology. Combinatorial work includes combinatorial commutative algebra, combinatorial matrix theory and random graphs and hypergraphs.

Research Area 02

Applied Mathematics

Applied mathematics is the department's founding tradition, growing out of Prof. B. R. Seth's school of continuum mechanics. Today the group develops mathematical models and computational methods for problems from science and engineering, often in collaboration with engineering departments across the Institute.

Active areas include fluid dynamics and transport phenomena — biofluid mechanics, low-Reynolds-number hydrodynamics, microfluidics, solute transport and Taylor dispersion, flow through porous media, water waves and double-diffusive convection; differential and integral equations and boundary value problems; numerical analysis and scientific computing, including finite-volume and higher-order schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws; and optimisation and operations research, from convex and numerical optimisation to portfolio optimisation, supply-chain logistics and fuzzy decision models.

Research Area 03

Probability & Statistics

The probability and statistics group works on both the theory of random phenomena and the methods used to draw conclusions from data. The department's expertise in statistics, quality control and reliability dates back several decades, and the group today contributes to data science and statistical simulation across the Institute.

Research covers mathematical statistics and statistical inference, including estimation in restricted parameter spaces, change-point problems and classification; applied probability and stochastic modelling, including queueing theory and limit theorems for dependent models; reliability theory and measures of entropy; directional statistics and statistics on manifolds; functional data analysis; and biostatistics, including survival analysis and surrogate endpoints in clinical trials.

Research Area 04

Theoretical Computer Science

The department was among the first in India to bring mathematics and computing together, through its M.Tech. in Computer Science and Data Processing and its Integrated M.Sc. in Mathematics and Computing. That tradition continues in a strong theoretical computer science group.

Research includes algorithms and computational complexity, computational geometry, computational topology and computational social choice; and an active cryptography group working on post-quantum and quantum cryptography, elliptic-curve and isogeny-based cryptography, algebraic and time/memory trade-off cryptanalysis, zero-knowledge proofs, functional encryption, key pre-distribution and blockchain and cryptocurrency. The group also connects with quantum computing, artificial intelligence and cyber security research across the Institute.
